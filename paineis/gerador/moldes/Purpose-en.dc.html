Why it does not pass the classic benchmarks

They ask a different question from the one it was built to answer.

anomaly benchmarks (NAB, SMD, SMAP, MSL) Sismo the question Is this moment an anomaly? What changed, in which field, since when, by how much, and does the rule from inputs to outcome still hold? a lasting change nobody labeled counts as a false alarm goes to the log, with its trajectory what you do next investigate the incident given the real outcomes: retrain if the rule broke, keep the model if only the inputs moved (intended use, not tested here)
⟦p1_k⟧ of ⟦p1_n⟧ labeled NAB events it finds, of those after its warm-up; its other warnings, ⟦p1_fp⟧ per ⟦mil⟧ items, fall outside the labels
⟦emb_zero⟧ warnings on shuffled copies of the same series: what it reports depends on the order of the data, which carries both changes and autocorrelation

In SMD, SMAP and MSL the null includes the training stretch, which those datasets do not guarantee to be free of change; if it is logging real changes there, the rule counts them against it. That is a hypothesis we have not yet checked; the autocorrelation of these series is at least as likely, since on stationary autocorrelated series with no change it also logs.

what it is for

It is meant to work next to an anomaly detector, a pairing this benchmark does not test. The anomaly detector says something is wrong now. Sismo says what changed, where, since when and by how much; it separates an alert from a log; and it keeps slow drift instead of throwing it away. None of these pieces is new; it puts them in one warning.

where it fell short on its own ground

It confirms a return to normal ⟦m5_h0⟧ to ⟦m5_h1⟧ hours after the recovery, and it found ⟦m1_k⟧ of ⟦m1_n⟧ contingencies in the simulated company. Both stay in the table.
